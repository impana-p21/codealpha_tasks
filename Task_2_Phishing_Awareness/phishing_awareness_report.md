# Phishing Awareness Training

## 1. Introduction

Phishing is a common form of cyber attack in which attackers pretend to be trustworthy individuals or organizations to trick victims into revealing sensitive information.

Attackers may attempt to steal:

- Usernames and passwords
- OTPs
- Banking information
- Credit/debit card details
- Personal information
- Login credentials

## 2. Common Types of Phishing

### Email Phishing

Attackers send fake emails that appear to come from legitimate organizations.

### Spear Phishing

A targeted phishing attack designed for a specific individual or organization.

### Smishing

Phishing attacks conducted through SMS or text messages.

### Vishing

Phishing attacks conducted through voice calls.

### Fake Websites

Attackers create websites that look similar to legitimate websites to collect login credentials.

## 3. How to Recognize Phishing

Common warning signs include:

1. Unknown or suspicious sender addresses.
2. Urgent or threatening language.
3. Unexpected attachments.
4. Suspicious links.
5. Requests for passwords or OTPs.
6. Poor spelling or grammar.
7. Unusual website addresses.
8. Offers that seem too good to be true.

## 4. Social Engineering

Social engineering involves manipulating people into performing actions or revealing information.

Attackers commonly use:

- Fear
- Urgency
- Authority
- Curiosity
- Trust
- Rewards

For example, an attacker may send a message claiming that an account will be blocked unless the victim immediately clicks a link.

## 5. Best Practices

Users should:

- Verify the sender before responding.
- Avoid clicking suspicious links.
- Check URLs carefully.
- Never share passwords or OTPs.
- Use strong and unique passwords.
- Enable multi-factor authentication.
- Keep software updated.
- Avoid opening unexpected attachments.
- Report suspicious messages.

## 6. Real-World Example

A user receives an email claiming to be from a bank.

The message says:

> "Your account will be suspended today. Click the link below to verify your account."

The link leads to a fake login page designed to steal the user's username and password.

### Warning Signs

- Urgent language
- Threat of account suspension
- Unexpected request
- Suspicious link
- Request for sensitive information

### Safe Action

The user should not click the link. Instead, they should independently visit the official banking website or contact the bank through an official channel.

## 7. Interactive Quiz

### Question 1

You receive an unexpected email asking you to urgently reset your password using a link.

**Should you click the link?**

Answer: No.

### Question 2

Should you share an OTP with someone who calls claiming to be from a bank?

Answer: No.

### Question 3

What should you do when you receive a suspicious email?

Answer: Verify the sender, avoid clicking links or attachments, and report the message if necessary.

## 8. Conclusion

Phishing attacks rely heavily on human mistakes and social engineering.

Awareness, careful verification, strong passwords, multi-factor authentication, and safe browsing practices can significantly reduce the risk of becoming a phishing victim.
